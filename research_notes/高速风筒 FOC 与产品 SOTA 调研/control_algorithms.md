## 1. Position/speed observers for high fundamental frequency and low carrier ratio

### Takeaway
The 2015–2026 literature agrees on *how* to make a back-EMF/flux observer survive low carrier ratios — design it directly in discrete time with a model that includes the PWM/computation delay, add explicit discretization/delay phase compensation, and make the PLL bandwidth speed-adaptive — but no head-to-head robustness benchmark of EEMF vs. active-flux vs. nonlinear flux observer vs. SMO at ≥100 krpm was found. The only angle-error figures at ≥100 krpm that surfaced are "<5° at 100 krpm" (100 W CSI-fed prototype, 2026 preprint) and a qualitative "no loss of accuracy" for an EKF up to 240 krpm (2022).

### Cited Findings
**Reviews / problem statement**
- Volpato Filho, Xiao, Vieira, Emadi (IEEE Access, 2021), "Observers for High-Speed Sensorless PMSM Drives: Design Methods, Tuning Challenges and Future Trends": reviews the stability constraints and design methods of the main high-speed estimators, then newer trends for non-ideal conditions — robustness to distortions, parameter variation, sensorless parameter estimation, and low sampling-frequency-to-speed-ratio operation. 105 Scopus citations per the HKUST portal. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/b2840c2911d74d999c6d67900d026a69); [HKUST portal](https://researchportal.hkust.edu.hk/en/publications/observers-for-high-speed-sensorless-pmsm-drives-design-methods-tu/)
- 夏长亮、叶梦婷 (浙江大学),《高速电机无位置传感器控制关键技术与前景展望》, 中国电机工程学报 2024, 44(17): 6991–7006, DOI 10.13334/j.0258-8013.pcsee.241256: starts from the high fundamental frequency and low inductance of high-speed machines; names three core problems — strongly coupled model, low carrier ratio, high stator-current harmonics; organises the field into modelling/decoupling, drive topology, and sensorless algorithms (position estimation + compensation). — [NAPSTIC record](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010zgdjgcxb202417020)
- Stated failure mechanisms at low carrier ratio (from the introduction of Bai et al., Sensors 2025): adding filters increases control delay and degrades filtering; in sliding-mode observers the extra delay lowers the effective switching frequency of the sign function and worsens chattering; in the EEMF method the resolution of current/voltage signals falls, hurting EMF accuracy. — [Sensors 2025, 25(5), 1290](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/5/1290)

**EEMF / back-EMF observers with discretization and delay compensation**
- Bai et al., Sensors 2025, DOI 10.3390/s25051290: refined EEMF estimator with discretization compensation, high-frequency harmonic filtering and real-time PLL-bandwidth adjustment, plus an "improved PR" controller that gives a streamlined calculation of the phase-lead angle for delay compensation; experiments on a 90 000 rpm, 30 kW motor on a DSP platform; reports better position/speed estimation than conventional EEMF. Numeric angle error and switching frequency were not in the retrievable text. — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/5/1290)
- Yang & Chen, IEEE TIE 2017 (DOI 10.1109/TIE.2017.2652364): high-speed PM machine (>10 000 r/min) sensorless drive using (1) a discrete-time dq current observer to remove inductive cross-coupling from the EMF estimate, (2) a delay model to compensate voltage error, (3) a discretized model to estimate the sampled EMF; experimentally verified. — [Unpaywall record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FTIE.2017.2652364)
- Tuovinen et al., IEEE ECCE 2015: speed-adaptive full-order observer designed directly in discrete time from an exact discrete motor model including control delays; 6.7 kW SyRM drive; "drastic performance improvements … especially if the sampling frequency is relatively low compared to the fundamental frequency". — [index record (mapping unverified)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/3679323.htm)
- Zhang & Liu, J. Phys.: Conf. Simulation only. — [record](https://harvester.ues.edu.sv/vufind/ProQuestFSGRecord/3194235865/Description)
- A 2025 MDPI Electronics article notes observers work well at high sampling-to-fundamental ratio but lose accuracy as speed rises, and cites a discrete sliding-mode observer that remained stable at a carrier ratio of 10. — [Electronics 14(13), 2516](https://mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/13/2516)
- A "Song, Lee, Yao" high-speed PMSM study (380 V, 14 krpm machine, 9 kHz switching) that puts PWM and digital delay in the discrete model reports steady-state angle error "nearly zero", high-speed transient angle error within 1°, and robustness to ±30 % stator inductance and ±50 % stator resistance error. — [aggregator record (mapping unverified)](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/424f765fb67e3ba24ee27f8549f4095dedccb966)

**EKF**

**Six-step / BLDC sensorless at 100 krpm (Chinese)**
- 电气传动 paper (DOI 10.19457/j.1001-2095.dqcd25893): ultra-high-speed brushless motor with front-end Buck converter + sensorless square-wave drive; back-EMF filter circuit and optimised commutation-compensation angle extend the sensorless range to 3 000–100 000 r/min; motivation is rotor/stator overheating from PWM-induced high-frequency current harmonics in low-inductance motors. — [电气传动 PDF](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/dqcd/EN/PDF/10.19457/j.1001-2095.dqcd25893)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc] Delay is the dominant angle-error source for this plant, not parameters.** One PWM period is 360°/N electrical: 33.0° at 20 kHz (N = 10.9), 20.6° at 32 kHz, 16.5° at 40 kHz, 10.3° at 64 kHz (N = 34.9). The classical 1.5·Ts sample-compute-update delay is therefore 49.5° / 30.9° / 24.8° / 15.5°. Any observer fed with commanded voltages that are not rotated forward by this angle (and with currents not referred to the correct sampling instant) inherits an error of this order — which is exactly what the discretization/delay compensations of Bai 2025, Yang & Chen 2017 and Tuovinen 2015 address.
- **[plant calc] Sensitivity to parameter error is intrinsically mild here.** With id = 0, R·iq (8.05 V) is in phase with the back-EMF (65.7 V), so an Rs error changes the estimated EMF/flux *amplitude* by ≈ 12 % × (relative R error) and the angle only to second order. An Lq error rotates the estimate by ≈ δLq·iq/ψ: Lq·iq/ψ = 0.147, so a 30 % Lq error gives ≈ 2.5° at rated current. Saturation-induced Ld ≠ Lq (0.30 vs 0.635 mH) therefore matters far less than a single uncompensated sample of delay.
- **[plant calc] Cost of angle error:** at fixed torque, copper loss scales ≈ 1/cos²δ: +3 % (≈ 0.5 W) at 10°, +13 % (≈ 2.1 W) at 20°, +33 % (≈ 5.3 W) at 30° — so the 5°-class accuracy reported at 100 krpm is the relevant target.
- **Cortex-M0 feasibility.** The optimised EKF cited above still needs 37.7 µs on a Cortex-M3, i.e. longer than one PWM period at ≥ 27 kHz; an EKF in the PWM interrupt is therefore not realistic on a Cortex-M0 at this plant's PWM rates, whereas back-EMF/flux observer + PLL structures (a 2-state integrator, one norm, one PLL) are the class that the cited ≥ 90 krpm experiments and the open-source firmware actually use. The 240 krpm EKF and the 90 krpm EEMF results were obtained on DSP-class hardware.
- **[BK-unverified]** The Lee-2010 observer as commonly written is x̂̇ = v − R·i + (γ/2)·η·(ψ² − |η|²), η = x̂ − L·i, θ̂ = atan2(η_β, η_α): no speed input, parameters R, L, ψ only. Not re-checked against the paper in this session.

### Gaps
- No source was found that compares EEMF, active-flux, nonlinear flux observer, SMO/STSMO, Luenberger and EKF on the same ≥ 100 krpm bench with injected parameter error, delay and saturation. "Most robust" could not be established from retrieved data.
- Active-flux observers, frequency-adaptive filters (SOGI-FLL, adaptive notch, complex-coefficient filters) and quadrature-PLL variants at high speed: not searched (budget).
- Numeric angle errors for Bai 2025 (90 krpm) and Niedermayr 2022 (240 krpm), and the carrier ratios of both, are in the full texts, which could not be opened.
- No published angle-error data were found for a ~100 W, 110 krpm, mains-fed, Cortex-M0, single-shunt drive (the team's exact class).
- The original VESC source (`foc_observer`/`mcpwm_foc`) and default γ values were not inspected.

## 2. Current control at low carrier ratio

### Takeaway
The established answer is a current regulator designed directly in the z-domain from the complex-vector plant model including the one-sample delay (Lorenz/Briz/Kim line; 2DOF and active-damping refinements up to a 2025 IEEE TPEL paper aimed specifically at sensorless ultra-high-speed PMSMs); deadbeat/predictive control is faster but parameter-sensitive, and plain delay compensation is reported to remain problematic at very low ratios. Quantitative bandwidth/stability limits did not surface in the retrievable text.

### Cited Findings
- Briz, Degner, Lorenz (IEEE TIA, 2000), "Analysis and design of current regulators using complex vectors" — origin of the complex-vector PI. — [WEMPEC record](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/1ee8b0b2-5c2e-4e39-933c-b75cb8009b07/full)
- Kim, Degner, Guerrero, Briz, Lorenz (IEEE TIA 2010; ECCE 2009, pp. 1317–1324), "Discrete-Time Current Regulator Design for AC Machine Drives": analyses regulators at high fundamental-to-sampling frequency ratios and designs a discrete-time complex-vector PI from a discrete-time plant model. Huh & Lorenz (2007) give the discrete-time model/design method as a generalisation of complex-vector pole-zero cancellation that also compensates the control delay. — [index record (mapping unverified)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1668802.htm)
- Bae & Sul (2003) is cited as the classic reference for compensating time delay in fully digital synchronous-frame regulators. — [Machines 2025, 13(11), 995](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/13/11/995/xml)
- Wang et al. (Zhejiang Univ., Frontiers of IT & EE, 2018, DOI 10.1631/FITEE.1601390): accurate two-degree-of-freedom discrete-time current controller designed from the exact complex-vector discrete model of a non-salient PMSM; 2.5 kW bench. — [journal page](https://www.engineering.org.cn/en/10.1631/FITEE.1601390)
- Li et al. (IEEE TPEL, 2025): "An improved discrete-time complex vector current regulator for position-sensorless ultra-high-speed PMSMs" using enhanced active damping for low switching-to-fundamental frequency ratios. Abstract-level only; no numbers retrieved. — [BIT portal](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/zh/publications/an-improved-discrete-time-complex-vector-current-regulator-for-po/)
- Dai et al. (IEEE TIE, 2022): deadbeat predictive control for high-speed PMSM at low switching-to-fundamental ratio using a stationary-frame stator-flux model that accounts for control delay and rotor movement; the paper models control delay as a first-order lag of 1.5·Ts and states that current delay compensation "still leaves stability problems at very high speeds with very low ratios"; simulation + prototype show gains over FOC and conventional deadbeat. — [White Rose postprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/174489/1/21-TIE-0250_final_submission.pdf)
- Walz et al. (IEEE Access, 2019), "Dahlin-based fast and robust current control of a PMSM in case of low carrier ratio": for fS/fel < 10 a high current-control bandwidth is important; deadbeat gives very fast dynamics but is weak under parameter mismatch; a Dahlin controller is proposed as the compromise between performance and stability; three schemes compared. — [Nottingham ePrints](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/64164)
- Lu et al. (中国电机工程学报, 2025, DOI 10.13334/j.0258-8013.pcsee.231871): "precise" deadbeat current control valid at both high and low carrier ratio, correcting rotor-flux-orientation error and dq coupling; experiments show better decoupling and tracking at low carrier ratio. — [CSEE](https://epjournal.csee.org.cn/zgdjgcxb/article/doi/10.13334/j.0258-8013.pcsee.231871)
- Machines 2025: conventional deadbeat predictive current control suffers large prediction error and torque–flux coupling at low carrier ratio; "excessively low carrier ratios may even lead to current loop instability"; one-step delay compensation is the standard remedy. — [Machines 13(11), 995](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/13/11/995/xml)
- Wang et al., variable-switching-frequency deadbeat predictive current control for high-speed/low-carrier-ratio PMSM: improves dynamic and steady-state behaviour without raising the carrier ratio; snippet mentions an 8 000 r/min simulation at 5 kHz. — [record](https://mdev.sciencepod.net/?p=571164)
- World Electric Vehicle Journal 2023: discrete current-error compensation for SPMSM at high speed and low carrier ratio. — [WEVJ 14(3), 69](https://mdpi.com/2032-6653/14/3/69)
- Politecnico di Bari (2024): voltage distortion due to digital delay grows with the ratio of electrical speed to control sampling frequency, affecting high-speed PMSMs in particular. — [postprint](https://iris.poliba.it/retrieve/handle/11589/255920/762886/2024_Parameter_Estimation_of_Isotropic_PMSMs_postprint.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc] This plant's current-loop problem is atypical: it is resistance-dominated.** ωe·Ld = 3.5 Ω and ωe·Lq = 7.3 Ω against R = 6.1 Ω, and L/R = 49–104 µs is of the order of one PWM period (50 µs at 20 kHz, 15.6 µs at 64 kHz). The open-loop plant pole (R/L ≈ 1.5–3.2 kHz) is thus naturally fast and well damped, unlike the low-R machines assumed in most low-carrier-ratio papers; cross-coupling (ωL·i ≈ 9.7 V at rated iq) is ≈ 13 % of the terminal voltage. Exact discrete-time decoupling still matters because the *frame rotation during the delay* (15–50°, see §1) mis-orients the applied voltage vector regardless of R.
- **[BK-unverified]** Standard delay-limited rule (Holmes et al., IEEE TPEL 2009): crossover ωc ≈ (π/2 − φm)/(1.5·Ts), i.e. fc ≈ fs/12 at 45° phase margin for single-update PWM, ≈ fs/6 with double update. **[plant calc]** That would give ≈ 1.7 / 3.3 / 5.3 kHz at 20 / 40 / 64 kHz single-update — at 20 kHz the achievable crossover is below the 1.83 kHz fundamental, which is the practical reason synchronous-frame regulation with exact discrete-time rotation/delay compensation (not stationary-frame tracking) is required.
- **[BK-unverified]** Hinkkanen, Awan, Qu, Tuovinen, Briz (IEEE TIA 2016) "Current control for synchronous motor drives: direct discrete-time pole-placement design" is the usual reference for 2DOF direct z-domain design on salient machines; it was searched for but not returned.
- Cortex-M0 cost: a discrete-time complex-vector PI differs from a classical PI + feedforward only by constant complex coefficients (functions of ωe·Ts, updated at the speed-loop rate), so its per-interrupt cost is essentially that of ordinary PI; deadbeat needs accurate L and ψ and is the method the sources flag as parameter-sensitive.

### Gaps
- Double sampling/double update, multi-sampling/oversampling and their measured bandwidth gains: not searched (budget).
- No retrieved source gave a measured current-loop bandwidth, or a minimum stable carrier ratio, for a discrete-time complex-vector PI on a PMSM; the "stable at ratio 10" statement found concerns an observer, not the current loop.
- Li et al. TPEL 2025 (most on-topic paper) could not be opened: speed, ratio and results unknown.
- Nothing was found on current-loop design for resistance-dominated miniature high-speed machines (R comparable to ωL) specifically.

## 3. What ultra-high-speed drives do in practice (ETH/Celeroton, Dyson, hair-dryer/vacuum drives): FOC vs block commutation with PAM

### Takeaway
The ETH/Celeroton lineage chose block (six-step) commutation with a front-end DC-DC stage (PAM) for 100 W–1 kW, 500 krpm machines because it minimises drive volume and avoids high PWM frequency on very low inductance, and Celeroton's commercial converters offer both PAM and PWM; Dyson's hair-dryer/vacuum motors are single-phase with table-driven commutation timing synchronised to the mains. Mass-market 105–110 krpm hair dryers today nevertheless ship three-phase sensorless FOC on Cortex-M0-class MCUs (teardown and vendor evidence), with vendor-claimed start times of 0.5–1 s.

**ETH Zurich / Celeroton**
- Zwyssig, Round, Kolar (IEEE TIE, 2008): 500 000 r/min, 100 W drive; "a voltage source inverter with block commutation and an additional dc-dc converter is selected as the most optimal choice" because it gives the lowest drive volume. Zwyssig, Duerr, Hassler, Kolar (PCC 2007): 1 kW, 500 000 rpm machine driven by a VSI with additional DC-DC converter, sensorless rotor-position detection and digital control. — [index records (mapping unverified)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/2847234.htm); [second record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/1053858.htm)
- Krähenbühl, Zwyssig, Weser, Kolar (ECCE 2009), miniature turbocompressor: bidirectional pulse-amplitude-modulation inverter with an extra buck converter; inverter in six-step/block commutation; sensorless position detection from the stator voltages. — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/doc/256434858/Zwyssig-Krahenbuhl-ECCE09-pdf)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc]** The team's plant is poorly matched to plain PWM from a raw mains bus: the 110 krpm back-EMF needs only |v| ≈ 74 V peak phase, i.e. modulation index ≈ 0.35–0.41 of the SVPWM linear limit on a 310–373 V bus. During zero vectors the phase current slews at e/L ≈ 65.7 V / (0.30–0.635 mH) ≈ 100–220 A/ms, so with ~15 µs of zero vector per half-period at 20 kHz the ripple is ≈ 1.5–3.3 A p-p — larger than the 1.32 A fundamental peak — falling to ≈ 0.5–1.0 A p-p at 64 kHz (rough estimate, ignores R and exact vector sequence). This is the same physics that led ETH/Celeroton to PAM (bus matched to back-EMF, switching at fundamental frequency) and that pushes FOC hair-dryer designs to tens-of-kHz PWM.
- The evidence set suggests two viable "SOTA" architectures for ~100 W / 110 krpm: (a) three-phase sensorless FOC at high PWM frequency on an M0-class MCU (what Laifen-class products ship), (b) block commutation with amplitude control (ETH/Celeroton; Dyson's single-phase variant uses conduction-timing control instead of a DC-DC stage). No retrieved source gives a measured loss/torque-ripple/cost comparison of (a) vs (b) at this power and speed.

## 4. DC-link ripple handling for rectified single-phase mains without PFC; small/medium DC-link capacitance; stability; IEC 61000-3-2

### Takeaway
The "small DC-link capacitor" literature (Jung et al. and Ha's group at SNU, Chinese CSEE/TPEL authors, Sheffield) shows that with only a few µF the inverter's output power directly shapes the grid current, so IEC/EN 61000-3-2 compliance and PF ≈ 0.97–0.98 are achieved by power/torque shaping plus active damping of the grid-L/film-C resonance, with rectified-voltage feedforward as the baseline. The team's 188 µF at ~130 W is not in that regime — it is a conventionally sized bus whose main control needs are bus-voltage feedforward and voltage-limit management at the ripple valleys; no source specific to that (medium-C, ~100 W) case was found.

### Cited Findings
- Jung et al. (single-phase-fed inverter with small DC-link capacitor): source-current harmonics meet IEC 61000-3-2 Class A and overall power factor is above 97 % without additional circuits such as an input filter. Power rating and test conditions not retrieved. — [index record (mapping unverified)](https://manuscript.isc.ac/inventory/49/77042.htm)
- A capacitorless single-to-three-phase inverter study reports that at rated speed and load the voltage-saturation region amounts to 40–50 % of the source period and the source-current harmonics do **not** meet IEC 61000-3-2; a direct DC-link current control is proposed to bring them under the limit. — [record (mapping unverified)](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/edb__86549282)
- Son & Ha (IEEE TPEL, 2015), "Direct power control of a three-phase inverter for grid input current shaping of a single-phase diode rectifier with a small DC-link capacitor": with a DC link of a few microfarads the grid-current shape is directly determined by inverter output power, so motor control and input-harmonic control are achieved together by motor-current reference generation plus direct output-power regulation through the voltage reference; harmonics below IEC 61000-3-2 limits and lower than the conventional method; electrolytic capacitor and input filter removed. APEC 2014 precursor (DOI 10.1109/APEC.2014.6803617, IPMSM and SynRM, no PFC); Lee, Son, Ha (IEEE TIA 2015) add a single-phase active-power-filter function. — [index record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/inventory/49/12485.htm)
- Yin et al. (Energies, 2020): improved sensorless control for IPMSM drives with small DC-link capacitors reduces estimation error; grid-current harmonics satisfy EN 61000-3-2. Yin et al. (IET Power Electronics, 2021, DOI 10.1049/pel2.12052): grid-current-feedback active damping raises power factor to 0.984 with EN 61000-3-2 compliance. — [Energies record](https://doaj.org/article/36ce8dc2f8ea441daa826964f89264f5); [IET PEL record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1049/pel2.12052)
- Ding et al. (IEEE TPEL, 2022), "Impedance Reshaping for Inherent Harmonics in PMSM Drives With Small DC Link Capacitor": addresses resonance excited by the dominant harmonics of a three-phase diode rectifier; validated on an electrolytic-capacitor-less PMSM prototype. — [DTU Orbit PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/280548016/Impedance_Reshaping_for_Inherent_Harmonics_in_PMSM_Drives_With_Small_DC_Link_Capacitor.pdf)
- Jun Yan (PhD thesis, Sheffield, 2024) and Yan et al. (IET Electric Power Applications, 2025): in small-DC-link drives energy backflow occurs when the back-EMF amplitude exceeds the minimum DC-link voltage; analysis and mitigation strategies. — [thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/35981/2/PhD%20thesis-2024-11-27-Jun%20Yan.pdf); [IET EPA 2025](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/228852/1/IET%20Electric%20Power%20Appl%20-%202025%20-%20Yan%20-%20Analysis%20and%20Mitigation%20Strategies%20of%20Energy%20Backflow%20in%20an%20IPMSM%20Drive%20System%20With.pdf)
- Chinese patent CN115378332B (无电解电容永磁同步电机控制方法): replacing the electrolytic with a small film capacitor makes the bus pulsate at twice mains frequency, and removing PFC makes the grid inductance resonate with the film capacitor; method adds a feedforward decoupling voltage to the current-loop PI output ("整流电压全前馈") and a bus-voltage-based power-compensation correction to meet IEC 61000-3-2 and raise input power factor. — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN115378332B/zh)
- 中国电机工程学报 paper (DOI 10.13334/j.0258-8013.pcsee.210235): frequency-domain model of the grid-side LC resonance's effect on drive admittance; extracts harmonic features of bus voltage to build a feedback loop that reshapes equivalent impedance and suppresses specific grid-side harmonics; verified on a 5.5 kW platform. — [CSEE](https://epjournal.csee.org.cn/zgdjgcxb/article/doi/10.13334/j.0258-8013.pcsee.210235)
- 高润峰 et al.: beat-frequency-amplitude-reconstruction impedance reshaping suppresses the motor-current beat amplitude to below 20 % in an electrolytic-capacitor-less drive; 丁大尉 et al. (中国电机工程学报 2025, vol. 45): grid-current harmonic suppression under unbalanced grid with rectified-voltage full feedforward. — [record (mapping unverified)](https://opaj.napstic.cn/periodicalArticle/0120250701446376)
- Dyson's alternative for single-phase motors (see §3): mains-zero-crossing-indexed lookup tables adjust advance/conduction/freewheel so that input-current shape gives "relatively high power factor … without PFC or high-capacitance link capacitor". — [US 2011/0254488](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20110254488)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc] Bus ripple of the team's drive (188 µF, assume ≈ 150 W drawn from the bus, discharge ≈ 8 ms at 50 Hz / 6.8 ms at 60 Hz):** Vvalley = √(Vpk² − 2·P·t/C). 230 Vac/50 Hz: 325 → ≈ 305 V (≈ 6 % ripple). 120 Vac/60 Hz: 170 → ≈ 134 V (≈ 21 %). 100 Vac/50 Hz: 141 → ≈ 85 V (≈ 40 %). At 1.3–1.4 µF/W this is a conventional peak-charging rectifier (short diode conduction), not a "small DC-link" drive; the inverter cannot shape the grid current the way the few-µF literature does, and the LC-resonance/negative-impedance instability those papers damp is correspondingly less of a concern (inference, not verified by a source for this capacitance).
- **[plant calc] The binding issue at low line is voltage, not harmonics.** Rated operation at id = 0 needs |v| = 74.3 V peak phase → Vdc ≥ 128.7 V for linear SVPWM (≥ 116.8 V for six-step), before dead-time/minimum-pulse losses. At the brief's 120 V bus minimum, linear SVPWM supports only ≈ 0.57 A iq (≈ 43 % of rated torque) at 110 krpm; the no-load requirement alone is 113.7 V. Below ≈ 114 V bus the line-line back-EMF exceeds the bus — the "energy backflow" condition analysed by Yan (2024/2025). Hence ripple-synchronous torque/power shaping (reduce iq at the valleys, recover at the crests), overmodulation, or a speed derate at low line is unavoidable; flux weakening is not an effective alternative for this motor (see §5).
- Bus-voltage feedforward in the modulator (duty = v*/Vdc measured each PWM period) is the baseline every cited scheme assumes ("整流电压全前馈"); with 100/120 Hz ripple and tens-of-kHz PWM the ripple is quasi-static per PWM period, so a per-period Vdc sample is adequate (inference).
- **[BK-unverified]** IEC 61000-3-2: equipment ≤ 75 W is exempt; a hair dryer is Class A, whose limits are absolute currents (3rd 2.30 A, 5th 1.14 A, 7th 0.77 A, …), which a ~130–150 W capacitor-input rectifier meets with a wide margin without any shaping; the standard's restrictions on asymmetrical/half-wave control (relevant to the heater, with a carve-out for short-duration hand-held appliances) are the more relevant clause for a complete hair dryer. None of this was retrieved or verified in this session.

### Gaps
- No paper was found on a *medium* DC link (~1–2 µF/W) at ~100 W with unregulated 100/120 Hz ripple feeding a 100 krpm motor; all quantitative results are for few-µF/kW-class film-capacitor drives (compressors, 5.5 kW benches).
- IEC 61000-3-2 limit values and class assignment were not retrieved from a citable source this session.
- Stability (negative incremental impedance / constant-power-load) margins as a function of capacitance and line inductance: only qualitative statements surfaced.
- Sul-group paper details (power level, capacitance, measured THD) could not be opened.

## 5. Dead-time and inverter nonlinearity; single-shunt reconstruction limits; overmodulation / six-step; flux weakening and MTPA / loss-minimising control (is id = 0 optimal?)

### Takeaway
Retrieved literature on single-shunt sensing in very-low-inductance machines says the measurement dead zones and the vector-shifting used to escape them are the main limitation (extra ripple, reconstruction error, even drive failure), and recent work (2024–2026) moves to averaged/observer-based reconstruction with dead-time feedforward. On id: generic loss-minimisation papers report up to 3.5 % efficiency gain over id = 0 on small motors, but nothing specific to a 2-pole 100 krpm SPM was found; arithmetic on the team's parameters indicates id = 0 is within ~0.2 W of optimal and that flux weakening is essentially unavailable (ψ/Ld ≈ 19 A vs ≈ 1.3 A rated).

### Cited Findings
**Single-shunt reconstruction**
- "Expanding Limit of Minimum Sampling Time Using Auxiliary Vectors for PMSM Drives with Single DC Link Current Sensor": the minimum sampling time depends on current-loop hardware and includes dead time, shoot-through protection and settling delays; phase-current-reconstruction precision is largely affected by unmeasurable zones and time-sharing sampling errors; the auxiliary-vector approach fails in the low-modulation area because two phase currents are not available at the same time. — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/189210/1/Expanding_Limit_of_Minimum_Sampling_Time_Using_Auxiliary_Vectors_for_PMSM_Drives_with_Single_DC_Link_Current_Sensor.pdf)
- HIT (IEEE TPEL, 2025), ultralocal-model generalized-integrator-observer-based current reconstruction: single-DC-link-sensor drives have a current-sampling dead region at high modulation index caused by topology and minimum sampling time. — [HIT portal](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/ultralocal-model-generalized-integrator-observer-based-current-re/)
- "Average Current Reconstruction Method for Ultra-Low Inductance PMSM Using a Single DC-Link Current Sensor" (IEEE Access, 2025): voltage injection to escape dead zones "generates additional current ripples, which further worsen current reconstruction error"; in ultra-low-inductance motors the intensified ripple "can ultimately lead to drive failures in low-speed operating regions"; alternative: sequential two-phase excitation with RC averaging. Related IEEE TIE 2024 work: "Ultra-Low Inductance PMSM Drive Using a DC Current Sensor". — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/b933be51d53443e58e0ab319e648e7e6); [Ewha portal](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/dc-current-sensing-drive-scheme-for-ultra-low-inductance-pmsms-wi/)
- Applied Sciences (2026): existing sensorless current-estimation methods are significantly affected by dead-time effects; proposed method identifies stator-current polarity and feeds forward a dead-time voltage correction in the αβ frame before reconstructing the DC-link current. — [Appl. Sci. 16(10), 5087](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/10/5087)
- TI application report SPRACT7 was returned for single-shunt FOC; contents not retrieved. — [TI SPRACT7](https://edgeworker.ti.com/lit/pdf/spract7)

**id = 0 vs loss-minimising / flux-weakening current**
- Online loss-minimisation (copper + iron) drive study: choosing id by speed and load gave "enhancement up to 3.5 % of the efficiency" over a traditional strategy, on small motors. — [UniPA paper](https://iris.unipa.it/bitstream/10447/30839/1/IES-PMSM.pdf)
- Because the loss model has no simple closed-form optimum, a golden-section search is used to find the maximum-efficiency operating point (IPMSM drive-system optimisation). — [UTS repository](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/177219/2/Multi-objective_Design_Optimization_of_an_IPMSM_Drive_System_Based_on_Loss_Minimization_Control_Strategy.pdf)
- Surface-PM machines: rotor is non-salient so d-axis armature reaction is much more sinusoidal than in interior machines; the smaller the magnet flux, the less d-axis flux is required in field weakening and the lower the harmonic iron losses. — [Adelaide thesis](https://drmc.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/bitstream/2440/44793/1/hdl_44793.pdf)
- 2003 SPMSM paper: flux-weakening current chosen for minimum copper loss, which differs from the maximum-torque and constant-power designs. — [NYCU repository](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/bitstream/11536/27751/1/000184375300001.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc] MTPA from the saturation saliency is negligible.** id,MTPA ≈ −(Lq − Ld)·iq²/ψ = −0.335 mH × (1.32 A)² / 5.7 mWb ≈ −0.10 A (current angle ≈ 4.4°); the torque-per-ampere gain is ≈ 0.3 %, i.e. ≈ 0.1 W of copper loss.
- **[plant calc] Iron-loss-minimising id is also negligible, under a simple model.** Assuming fundamental iron loss ∝ |λs|², each −1 A of id reduces d-axis flux by Ld/ψ = 5.3 % but costs 1.5·R·id² = 9.15 W/A² of copper loss. The optimum is id* ≈ −0.0057 A per watt of iron loss, saving ≈ 3×10⁻⁴·Pfe² W: for Pfe = 10 W, id* ≈ −0.06 A and the saving is ≈ 0.03 W; for 20 W, ≈ −0.12 A and ≈ 0.12 W. So for this high-R, low-Ld motor id = 0 is loss-optimal to within ≈ 0.1–0.2 W (≈ 0.1 % of output) — an order of magnitude less than the loss caused by a 10–20° angle error (§1). The model ignores PWM-harmonic iron/rotor loss, which id does not affect anyway.
- **[plant calc] Flux weakening is ineffective.** Characteristic current ψ/Ld ≈ 19 A vs ≈ 1.3 A rated. id = −1 A lowers |v| only from 74.3 V to 72.0 V (−3 %) while adding ≈ 9 W of copper loss (R·id appears in vd). Voltage shortfall at bus valleys must therefore be handled by torque/speed shaping or by overmodulation toward six-step (+10 % fundamental over linear SVPWM: 2/π·Vdc vs Vdc/√3), not by negative id.
- **[plant calc] Dead-time error is first-order here.** Per-leg average error ΔV = td·fPWM·Vdc: with an assumed td = 0.5 µs and Vdc = 310 V, ΔV = 3.1 V at 20 kHz, 6.2 V at 40 kHz, 9.9 V at 64 kHz — comparable to the whole resistive drop R·iq = 8 V and ≈ 5–15 % of the back-EMF, and it enters the observer's voltage model directly. With current ripple (§3) larger than the fundamental, phase currents cross zero several times per fundamental cycle, so sign-based compensation is ill-defined; this is consistent with the 2026 Applied Sciences finding that dead time dominates reconstruction/estimation error.
- **[plant calc] Single-shunt windows are tight.** At m ≈ 0.35–0.41 and 40–64 kHz the active-vector dwell times are a few µs, of the same order as dead time + amplifier settling + ADC sampling quoted as the "minimum sampling time" in the cited papers, so vector shifting (and its extra ripple in a 0.3–0.6 mH motor) would be needed over much of the electrical cycle — the failure mode the 2025 IEEE Access paper describes for ultra-low-inductance motors.

### Gaps
- Overmodulation, six-step transition and sensorless operation in the overmodulation region: the search for these was not executed (budget). Nothing can be reported.
- Flux-weakening control for sensorless high-speed SPMSM (voltage-feedback methods, behaviour under bus ripple): not searched.
- No retrieved paper quantifies dead-time compensation accuracy at carrier ratios of 10–35, nor iron-loss-optimal id for a 2-pole 100 krpm slotless/SPM machine; the iron-loss split of the team's motor is unknown, so the id-optimality conclusion above rests on an assumed loss model.
- Quantitative single-shunt limits (minimum measurable window vs PWM frequency) for this class were not found in retrievable text.

## 6. Startup: I/F open-loop with smooth closed-loop handover, initial position detection, flying start

### Takeaway
I/F (current-controlled open-loop) start with a compensated handover to the EMF observer remains the standard for sensorless drives of this type; the one ≥ 100 krpm paper found (10 kW, 100 000 r/min, APEC 2022) adds observer voltage compensation and an adaptive torque-reference transition but is simulation-level. Flying start is typically done either with one-to-three zero-vector short-circuit pulses or by simply running the back-EMF estimator; the best "reported" start times are vendor claims of 0.5–1 s to ~110 krpm.

### Cited Findings
- Fatu, Teodorescu, Boldea, Andreescu, Blaabjerg (Aalborg): I-f starting method with smooth transition to EMF-based motion-sensorless vector control of PMSM; a first-order lag compensator ensures the switch-over. — [AAU portal](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/i-f-starting-method-with-smooth-transition-to-emf-based-motion-se)
- BIT (IEEE APEC 2022): "I/F starting rapid and smooth transition method of full-speed sensorless" control for a 100 000 r/min, 10 kW ultra-high-speed PMSM — I-f start for fast stable acceleration, extended back-EMF observer with voltage compensation, transition governed by adaptive gradient descent on the reference electromagnetic torque; simulation shows reduced position-estimation error and a better transition than the traditional method. — [BIT portal](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/i-f-starting-rapid-and-smooth-transition-method-of-full-speed-sen)
- Sensors 2023: I/F start + extended Kalman filter hybrid for full-speed sensorless control, with damping compensation to reduce speed oscillation during I/F and switch-over at a speed threshold. — [PMC9861642](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9861642/)
- 2025 arXiv (CSI-fed submersible-pump drives): I-f start-up can misalign the synchronous and actual rotor frames under high start-up currents; a first-order compensator is the known remedy for aligning the frames. — [arXiv 2503.22855](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.22855)
- MathWorks Motor Control Blockset example: switches from I-f to sensorless FOC at ≈ 15 % of base speed and back to I-f at ≈ 7.5 % (hysteresis). imperix TN137: I-f start-up shares the FOC current controller. — [MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/mcb/gs/sensorless-foc-of-pmsm-using-if-ctrl-startup.html); [imperix TN137](https://imperix.com/doc/TN137)
- Flying start via zero-voltage-vector short circuit: the stator is short-circuited by a zero vector and speed/position are extracted from the short-circuit current; methods are classified as single, double, triple or repetitive short-circuit — more pulses broaden the applicable range and improve accuracy but slow the torque response after power returns. — [Energies 2022, 15(9), 3469](https://mdpi.com/1996-1073/15/9/3469)
- Flying start without extra injection: re-use the existing back-EMF estimator during restart. — [Energies 2019, 12(9), 1818](https://mdpi.com/1996-1073/12/9/1818)
- IEEE SLED 2023: magnet-polarity identification for flying start with a single zero-voltage-vector pulse, validated on a 2.2 kW PMSM drive. — [SLED 2023 abstract](https://di0zxmb8pwajl.cloudfront.net/ieee-sled/conference/sled2023/programBook/abs/0032F.pdf)
- 2019 Korean paper: extended-EMF-based restart, initial speed/angle from the short-circuit current of a zero voltage vector. — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201914260133959.pdf)
- Initial position by pulse injection: a 2010 IEEE Trans. Magnetics paper uses DC voltage pulses for initial position and polarity with a nonlinear model including saturation saliency; a review notes the pulse method has small theoretical error but pulse width/amplitude are hard to choose; a 2023 paper notes saturation-based polarity detection is unreliable for low-saturation machines and proposes torque-pulse injection. — [TMAG 2010 record (mapping unverified)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/3458066.htm); [WEVJ 2020, 11(4), 71](https://mdpi.com/2032-6653/11/4/71)
- Vendor claims for hair-dryer drives: standstill to top speed in 0.5 s (BPI/Fortior, 60–110 W, 110 krpm class); start within 1 s and stop within 200 ms at 110 000 rpm (Arrow/ST 2021). — [SMZDM](https://post.smzdm.com/p/awz4n602/); [ST/Arrow deck](https://www.st.com/content/dam/is21/pdf/MC1_05_Arrow_BLDC_hair_dryer_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc]** With ψ = 5.7 mWb the back-EMF is only 0.6 V peak per 1 000 rpm, while R·i at rated current is 8 V; the EMF equals the resistive drop only near 13–14 krpm. Dead-time error (3–10 V, §5) is larger than the EMF below roughly 5–17 krpm, so an EMF/flux observer cannot be trusted until well above that — the I/F stage must carry the rotor to roughly 10–15 % of rated speed, consistent with the 15 % hand-over in the MathWorks example.
- For flying start in this plant the zero-vector short-circuit current is limited by R + jωL (6.1 Ω dominant at low speed), so pulse amplitudes are small and benign; alternatively the open-circuit back-EMF is large (65 V at speed), favouring "run the observer with zero current command" approaches (inference from the cited method classes, not a tested result).
- The saturation-induced saliency (Ld/Lq = 0.30/0.635 mH) suggests pulse-based initial position/polarity detection is physically possible, but the 2023 caution about saturation-based polarity reliability applies; no data for 2-pole slotless/SPM hair-dryer motors were found.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed start-up-time or start-success-rate statistics were found for 100 krpm-class appliance motors; the 0.5 s and 1 s figures are unverified vendor claims without test conditions.
- The 100 000 r/min APEC 2022 result is simulation-only as far as could be determined; no experimental ≥ 100 krpm handover data were retrieved.
- Accuracy (electrical degrees) and duration of pulse-injection initial-position detection: not available in retrievable text.
- Vendor start-up features (TI IPD/align/slow-first-cycle, ST MCSDK rev-up and on-the-fly start, Microchip AN1078/AN1292) were not retrieved.

## 7. Online parameter identification and sensorless temperature estimation

### Takeaway
The governing constraints are rank deficiency (steady-state dq equations cannot separate Rs, L and ψ without extra excitation or operating-point changes) and inverter-nonlinearity/delay voltage errors, both of which worsen at high speed; at medium/high speed the PM flux is the parameter worth adapting, while Rs adaptation is a low-speed/injection technique. No retrieved source gave a temperature-accuracy figure (in kelvin) for sensorless Rs- or ψ-based estimation at high speed.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Liang, Liu (IEEE Access, 2021), "Online Parameter Estimation for Permanent Magnet Synchronous Machines: An Overview": reviews online estimation with examples, covering the rank-deficient issue, inverter nonlinearities and thermal condition monitoring. — [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/173931/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/d72df6462d024764b66ccce0be77323f)
- Piippo, Hinkkanen, Luomi (IEEE TIA, 2009), "Adaptation of motor parameters in sensorless PMSM drives": stator-resistance adaptation uses high-frequency signal injection and works down to zero speed; the PM flux is estimated at medium and high speeds, "when the effect of the PM flux estimation error is more significant than that of the stator resistance estimation error"; when injection is removed at higher speed, sensitivity to parameter errors increases. — [Aalto postprint](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/4504376/j13_post.pdf)
- Politecnico di Bari (2024), parameter estimation of isotropic PMSMs: voltage distortion due to digital delay increases with the ratio of electrical speed to sampling frequency, penalising voltage-reference-based identification in high-speed PMSMs. — [postprint](https://iris.poliba.it/retrieve/handle/11589/255920/762886/2024_Parameter_Estimation_of_Isotropic_PMSMs_postprint.pdf)
- A high-speed-PMSM study notes multi-parameter estimation is hard because of the low resistance value and proposes a non-iterative method using multi-frequency disturbance injection to estimate R and L together. — [record (mapping unverified)](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c292t-a39df94c27a087509a93e5db3c2838f63ca5cd83ccaed119f8b9b1deee10971e3)
- Perera & Nilsen (arXiv, 2022): recursive prediction-error (gradient) methods estimate ψm and Rs online for an IPMSM, motivated by indirect temperature tracking; demonstrated with a position sensor, sensorless extension stated as possible. — [arXiv 2209.05094](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05094)
- Feuersänger (PCIM 2011): PM flux measured online to estimate rotor temperature, but with a mechanical encoder; temperature rise at rated operation can reach 100 K in industrial PMSMs, and Rs and ψ cannot be treated as standstill constants because of their temperature dependence. — [PCIM 2011 paper](https://www.eti.uni-siegen.de/lea/lel_ea/mitarbeiter/feuersaenger/pcim_2011.pdf)
- Xiao & Griffo (J. Eng. 2019; IEEE TPEL 2020, vol. 35 no. 6): PM flux linkage estimated online from the current response to the standard SVPWM pattern — no injected signal and no need for saturation-dependent inductances; experimentally verified and applied to rotor-temperature prediction. — [J. Eng. record](https://doaj.org/article/accca5986bd04e3e88eaeb902dd7b133); [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/152551/)
- Ganchev (TU Wien dissertation, 2013): magnet temperature estimated from the d-axis current slope under voltage pulses (temperature-dependent magnetisation changes d-axis saturation), with q-current compensation for cross-saturation. — [TU Wien](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/5596)
- Bernard & Praly (IFAC 2017): the gradient flux observer extended to estimate magnet flux online (see §1); MESC/VESC firmware includes a flux-linkage observer alongside the position observer. — [Bernard & Praly](https://cas.mines-paristech.fr/~praly/Telechargement/Conferences/2017_IFAC_Bernard-Praly.pdf); [MESC docs](https://davidmolony.github.io/MESC_Firmware/operation/CONTROL.html)
- Among recursive methods, RLS is reported to improve position-estimation accuracy in sensorless drives; EKF gives decent performance at higher computational burden. — [Kastamonu Univ. paper](https://unis.kastamonu.edu.tr/yayin-detay/2_D3aqD3S_31/an-adaptive-filter-based-online-parameter-identification-method-for-permanent-magnet-synchronous-motors/pdf=1)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc] Observability in this plant.** At id = 0 steady state the two equations are vd = −ωe·Lq·iq and vq = R·iq + ωe·ψ: Lq is identifiable from vd; R and ψ share one equation (rank deficiency per Zhu et al.). The plant is unusually favourable for separating them by load variation because R·iq (8 V) is 12 % of ωe·ψ (65.7 V) rather than the ~1 % typical of low-R high-speed machines — two operating points at the same speed with different iq (e.g. the bus-ripple-synchronous torque modulation of §4, which occurs naturally at 100/120 Hz) give the slope R and intercept ωe·ψ. Whether dead-time error (3–10 V, same size as R·iq) can be compensated well enough for this to work is untested.
- **[BK-unverified]** Copper resistance rises ≈ 0.39 %/K and NdFeB remanence falls ≈ 0.11–0.12 %/K, so a ±1 % error in ψ corresponds to roughly ±9 K of magnet temperature and a ±4 % error in R to roughly ±10 K of winding temperature; ψ is read from an amplitude of ≈ 66 V, so 1 % is 0.66 V — well below the uncompensated dead-time error. Voltage-error compensation, not the estimator algorithm, therefore sets the achievable temperature accuracy (inference).
- Cortex-M0 cost: slope/intercept regression or a scalar gradient/RLS update can run at the speed-loop rate (kHz or below), unlike the observer itself; EKF-based joint estimation is the heavy option per the cited comparison.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source reports winding- or magnet-temperature accuracy in kelvin for a *sensorless* drive at high speed, nor the operating conditions required; the 2022–2026 literature on this could not be searched in depth.
- In sensorless operation ψ error and angle error are coupled (the observer uses ψ); no quantified treatment of this coupling at ≥ 100 krpm was found.
- MRAS-based Rs/ψ identification results and DC-injection Rs estimation accuracy: not found in retrievable text.
- Magnet material of the team's motor is unknown, so the temperature coefficient to apply is not established.

## 8. Efficiency and acoustic optimisation

### Takeaway
Evidence on PWM-frequency/GaN benefits is real but scattered: measured motor-side gains from raising PWM from 20 to 100 kHz range from about 1 % (a 2024 GaN PMSM study) to "over 5 % system efficiency" (a 48 V GaN prototype with a high-speed outrunner), with small inverter-side penalties at low power; random/periodic switching-frequency modulation is consistently reported to reduce tonal high-frequency vibration/noise at the cost of possibly higher THD, but no dB figures surfaced. Harmonic-current suppression methods were not searched.

### Cited Findings
**PWM frequency, ripple-induced loss, wide-bandgap**
- TI TIDA-00909 (48 V GaN inverter reference design): "high-speed drives and/or low inductance brushless motors require higher inverter switching frequencies in the range of 40 kHz to 100 kHz to minimize losses and torque ripple in the motor"; measured inverter losses 4.95 W at 40 kHz vs 5.65 W at 100 kHz PWM. — [TI TIDUCE7](https://ti.com/lit/pdf/tiduce7)
- TI drone-ESC reference design: PWM tested up to 60 kHz, stated to reduce current/torque ripple for low-inductance high-speed motors. — [TI ZHCU503](https://www.ti.com/cn/lit/ug/zhcu503/zhcu503.pdf)
- 48 V GaN prototype with high-speed outrunner motor: system efficiency improved by over 5 % when switching at 100 kHz rather than 20 kHz, without significant impact on GaN HEMT loss or temperature; lower current ripple also reduces DC-link capacitance. — [candidate source (mapping unverified)](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2962063/578598)
- "PMSM Drive Loss Reduction by Applying GaN Transistor based Inverter" (2024): high switching frequency improves motor efficiency by approximately one percent while degrading inverter efficiency. — [RTU record](https://ortus.rtu.lv/science/en/publications/41580)
- Fraunhofer (PCIM 2023): simulation study comparing multilevel GaN inverters with standard B6 inverters for low-inductance 80 kW PMSM drives above 100 000 rpm. — [Fraunhofer Publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/506581)
- "The effect of PWM on rotor eddy-current losses in high-speed permanent magnet machines": PWM current harmonics sit at very high frequencies because of the high fundamental; a fast analytical method is given to include PWM-driven rotor loss at design stage; direct rotor-loss measurement is extremely difficult, especially with a conductive retaining sleeve, so few papers offer convincing prediction-vs-measurement comparisons. — [record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi.dedup.....88aecad63deb363b787adcbc4b979ff5)
- Chalmers paper: proposes adapting switching frequency with speed and torque; notes optimum switching frequency is usually computed from analytical models and manufacturer loss data, and that earlier work on a small GaN drive did not quantify results at drive-cycle level; higher switching frequency raises iron/eddy losses in stator and rotor together with converter switching loss in some regimes. — [Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/546713/file/546713_Fulltext.pdf)
- An, Lu, Zhao (Energies 2024 review): the small inductance of high-speed PM motors produces abundant high-frequency current harmonics. — [Energies 17(21), 5252](https://mdpi.com/1996-1073/17/21/5252)

**Random / spread-spectrum PWM**
- IEEE Access 2020, harmonic spread-spectrum technique for PMSM acoustic noise and vibration: random PWM spreads harmonic power over a wide spectrum; experiments show significant reduction of acoustic noise/vibration and torque ripple. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/d71d6ff2d102494895492418be4de39a)
- 2021 comparison of fixed, periodic, random and hybrid switching-frequency strategies: the three modulated strategies all suppress high-frequency current-harmonic amplitude significantly, with little difference in noise/vibration reduction among them; random and hybrid are best at reducing high-frequency vibration/noise amplitude. — [aggregator record](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/812359768994742273)
- 2023 arXiv preprint: selective noise suppression using random SVPWM — randomising both switching frequency and pulse position gives a more uniform PSD near multiples of the switching frequency and can null specified frequencies. — [arXiv 2302.08053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08053)
- IEEE Industry Applications Magazine 2022: random hysteresis current control vs pseudorandom triangular PWM, hysteresis and sinusoidal PWM — substantial reduction of acoustic noise and vibration. Caveat from a 2021 preprint: random PWM can increase THD. — [BIU portal](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/a-novel-application-of-random-hysteresis-current-control-acoustic/); [preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202107.0290/v1/download)

**Harmonic-current control**
- Bai et al. (Sensors 2025) use an improved PR controller with discretization compensation in a 90 krpm drive (see §1). — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/5/1290)

### Inferences
- **[plant calc]** For this plant the PWM-frequency lever is large: estimated ripple ≈ 1.5–3.3 A p-p at 20 kHz vs ≈ 0.5–1.0 A p-p at 64 kHz against a 1.32 A fundamental (§3). Treating the ripple as roughly triangular (rms ≈ p-p/2√3), ripple copper loss 3·R·Irms² is of order 3–17 W at 20 kHz and 0.4–1.5 W at 64 kHz (AC resistance, iron and rotor-eddy contributions not included, and the ripple estimate is coarse). Relative to a 15.9 W fundamental copper loss this indicates PWM frequency is a first-order efficiency parameter for this motor, in line with TI's "40–100 kHz for low-inductance high-speed motors" guidance.
- Random/spread-spectrum PWM interacts with everything in §1–§2 that assumes a constant Ts (discrete-time regulator coefficients, delay compensation angle = 1.5·ωe·Ts, single-shunt sampling windows); on a carrier ratio of 10–35 the delay angle changes by several degrees per period when Ts is dithered, so Ts-dependent terms would need per-period update (inference; no source found addressing random PWM at low carrier ratio).
- At 20–64 kHz PWM the switching tones are at or above the top of the audible band; the audible signature of a 110 krpm 2-pole motor is more likely the 1.83 kHz fundamental and its harmonics (mechanical/aerodynamic and current-harmonic), for which spread-spectrum PWM does nothing (inference).

### Gaps
- Harmonic-current suppression at low carrier ratio (resonant controllers, multiple synchronous frames, ADRC, repetitive control) and quantitative THD results: not searched (budget).
- No dB(A) figures for random PWM, and no acoustic study of 100 krpm appliance motors, were retrieved.
- No measured loss split (copper/iron/rotor-eddy vs PWM frequency) for a ~100 W, 0.3–0.6 mH, 110 krpm motor was found; no peer-reviewed measurement isolating motor-loss reduction from GaN/SiC at this class.
- GaN/SiC in mains-fed (300–400 V) hair-dryer/vacuum inverters: nothing retrieved.

## 9. Recent review papers (2021–2026) on (ultra-)high-speed PMSM sensorless control and their stated open problems

### Takeaway
Three reviews frame the field consistently — Volpato Filho et al. (IEEE Access 2021), An et al. (Energies 2024) and 夏长亮/叶梦婷 (中国电机工程学报 2024) — and all name the same triad of open problems: low carrier ratio (delay/discretization), high current harmonics from low inductance, and strongly coupled models, plus full-speed-range operation and reliability. No review dedicated to "ultra-high-speed + low carrier ratio + low-cost MCU" was found.

### Cited Findings
- Volpato Filho, Xiao, Vieira, Emadi (IEEE Access, 2021): observer design methods, tuning challenges and future trends for high-speed sensorless PMSM; future-trend topics include robustness to distortions and parameter variation, sensorless parameter estimation, and low sampling-frequency-to-speed-ratio operation. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/b2840c2911d74d999c6d67900d026a69)
- An, Lu, Zhao, "Review of Key Technologies of the High-Speed Permanent Magnet Motor Drive", Energies 2024, 17(21), 5252 (published 22 Oct 2024, DOI 10.3390/en17215252): analyses high-speed PM drive problems under three headings — current harmonics, rotor position detection, low carrier ratios — then reviews harmonic suppression, sensorless methods and control strategies, and discusses future trends. — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/1996-1073/17/21/5252)
- 夏长亮、叶梦婷,《高速电机无位置传感器控制关键技术与前景展望》, 中国电机工程学报 2024, 44(17): 6991–7006: challenges and outlook are harmonic suppression, operating reliability and full-speed-range high-performance algorithms; covers precise modelling/decoupling, drive topologies and position estimation/compensation. — [NAPSTIC](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010zgdjgcxb202417020)
- Zhu, Liang, Liu (IEEE Access, 2021): overview of online parameter estimation for PMSMs (rank deficiency, inverter nonlinearity, thermal monitoring). — [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/173931/)
- General sensorless reviews: "Overview of Position-Sensorless Technology for PMSM Systems" (World Electric Vehicle Journal 2023, 14(8), 212); 电机与控制应用 2024, 51(1): 1–13 (DOI 10.12177/emca.2023.165); 电子科技 2021, 34(4): 59–69 (zero/low-speed, medium/high-speed and full-range methods classified and compared). — [WEVJ](https://www.mdpi.com/2032-6653/14/8/212/xml); [电机与控制应用 record](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010zxxdj202401001); [电子科技](https://journal.xidian.edu.cn/dzkj/CN/10.16180/j.cnki.issn1007-7820.2021.04.010)
- Thesis-level source: "Research on Sensorless and Robust Control of High-Speed Surface-Mounted Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motors" (UTS). — [UTS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/195801)

### Inferences
- The reviews' open-problem list maps directly onto the team's design risks, in this order of quantitative weight for their plant (from the [plant calc] items above): delay/discretization at carrier ratio 10–35 (15–50° per 1.5 Ts) ≫ dead-time voltage error (3–10 V vs 8 V resistive drop) ≈ PWM ripple/harmonics (ripple ≥ fundamental at 20 kHz) ≫ inductance/saturation error (≈ 2.5° for 30 %) ≈ MTPA/LMC benefit (≈ 0.1–0.2 W).
- None of the reviews, as far as their abstracts show, addresses the combination that defines the team's product class: ~100 W, resistance-dominated miniature machine, unregulated rippling mains bus, single-shunt sensing and a fixed-point Cortex-M0. The published ≥ 90 krpm experimental results retrieved (30 kW/90 krpm, 240 krpm fuel-cell compressor, 10 kW/100 krpm, 100 W CSI/100 krpm) are on DSP-class controllers and stiff or regulated DC supplies.

### Gaps
- Conclusion and future-trend sections of all three reviews could not be read (hosts blocked); only abstract-level statements are reported.
- 电工技术学报 and 电机与控制学报 reviews, CES TEMS reviews, and any 2025–2026 IEEE review on ultra-high-speed sensorless control were not searched (budget) — their existence is not established here.
- No review or paper was found that reports sensorless FOC results on a Cortex-M0-class MCU at ≥ 100 krpm with quantitative angle error; industrial practice in that class is documented only by teardowns and vendor claims (§3).
