## 1. Chinese vendors dominant in hair dryers (Fortior, Linko, CMS and others): parts, claims, features, market share

### Takeaway
Teardowns from 2021 to 2026 show three Chinese MCU suppliers recurring in 100k–110k rpm mains hair dryers: Fortior FU6812L/FU6861 (8051 + hardware "ME" FOC engine), Linko LKS32MC03x/07x (now part of 晶丰明源 BPS, and found in the 2026 Dyson HD19), and CMS CMS32M5710 (64 MHz Cortex-M0, the low-cost choice in Laifen SE-class and white-label units). No hair-dryer-specific market-share figure was found for any vendor; the only share numbers are for the whole BLDC control-chip market or for 2020 vacuum/fan segments.

### Inferences
- The dominant Chinese architecture is an 8051+hardware-FOC or Cortex-M0 MCU running a vendor-supplied sensorless FOC library; none of the retrieved vendor material names the observer type (sliding-mode, flux, PLL), so observer design cannot be compared from public sources.
- Linko's own published dryer solution uses the 48 MHz LKS32MC037, not the 96 MHz 07x the team uses; Linko claims 220,000 electrical rpm capability for that smaller part, which is twice the team's 110,000 electrical rpm (1.83 kHz) requirement. The team's MCU is therefore above, not below, the vendor's reference compute tier for this application.
- Fortior's claimed 5.6 µs hardware FOC execution is the only published control-loop timing figure among the Chinese vendors; it implies the loop is not the speed bottleneck on that platform, but the figure is from 2020 vendor marketing and unverified.

### Gaps
- No hair-dryer-specific market-share statement was found for Fortior, Linko, CMS or anyone else; the 78–80% figures are 2020-era vacuum/fan or loosely defined "small-appliance BLDC" claims.
- Observer type, maximum electrical frequency guaranteed in the datasheet, start-up time in a dryer, and single/dual/triple-shunt options were not found in primary documents for Fortior FU6812/FU6861, Linko's library, or CMS; only the marketing-level claims above.
- Dedicated Fortior hair-dryer ASSPs (beyond FU6812/FU6861) and any Fortior 32-bit successors were not found.
- A headline "中微半导体推出基于CMS32M6710主控的24万转高速风筒方案" (CMS32M6710-based 240,000 rpm dryer solution) surfaced in search results, but the originating page was not identified and no specifications were retrieved; treat the CMS32M67xx dryer solution as unconfirmed.
- Not found (queries never ran or returned nothing): 雅特力 Artery, 兆易创新 GD32, 芯旺 KungFu, 南芯, 必易微 MCU-level solutions, MindMotion specifications, and 晶丰明源's own claims beyond the joint Linko solution.
- Comparator-to-PWM trip latency in nanoseconds was not found for any Chinese MCU.

## 2. LKS32MC07x specifically: core, clock, ADC, MCPWM fail inputs, comparators/DAC, DSP, flash, limits, and Linko's dryer claims

### Takeaway
LKS32MC07x is a 96 MHz Cortex-M0 with a proprietary motor-control DSP, dual simultaneous 3 Msps 12-bit ADCs, four op-amps, three comparators, two 12-bit DACs and an MCPWM block whose FAIL (emergency-stop) input can be driven from a pin or directly from the on-chip comparators with a programmable digital filter. It is the same family Dyson uses in the HD19 (LKS32MC072KBQ8), but Linko publishes no 07x hair-dryer solution or dryer-specific performance claim.

### Cited Findings
- Core and clock: 96 MHz 32-bit Cortex-M0 plus a motor-control DSP with Linko's own instruction set; internal 8 MHz RC oscillator with PLL up to 96 MHz. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet (LCSC copy, ©2023)](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- Memory: 64 KB or 128 KB flash, 12 KB SRAM. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- ADC: two 12-bit SAR ADCs, synchronous dual sampling, 3 Msps sampling and conversion rate, up to 16 channels each. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- Analog: four high-speed op-amps and three comparators; op-amp inputs have high-voltage protection so MOSFET on-resistance sensing can be connected directly; two 12-bit DACs; on-chip temperature sensor. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- MCPWM: two groups of six PWM outputs with configurable dead time. Communication: two UART, one SPI, one IIC. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- FAIL path: FAIL mode is entered when a designated IO pin is triggered externally or when a CMP comparator trips; the pin table maps comparator outputs (CMP0_OUT, CMP1_OUT, CMP2_OUT) and stop inputs MCPWM_BKIN0/1. On FAIL, or when MOE = 0, outputs go to a configured default level. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf); [CSDN blog on LKS PWM configuration](https://blog.csdn.net/nullccc/article/details/149409725)
- FAIL filtering: the emergency-stop signal passes a digital filter clocked from MCPWM_TCLK; example code sets CMP_FLT_CLKDIV = 12, and a separate field IO_CMP_FLT_CLKDIV covers pin/comparator sources. A blog gives comparator filter width = tclk period × 16 × CMP_FltCnt, with CMP_FltCnt from 0 to 15. — [21ic LKS32MC071CBT8 board review, part 6](https://bbs.21ic.com/icview-3310138-1-1.html); [CSDN blog on LKS PWM configuration](https://blog.csdn.net/nullccc/article/details/149409725); [LKS32MC07x User Manual EN v1.65](https://sjk.lksmcu.com/static/upload/file/20230113/LKS32MC07x_User_Manual_EN_v1.65.pdf)
- Comparator delay: the datasheet is reported as giving a comparator delay selectable by register CMP_FT of "<30 ns / 200 ns"; the retrieved snippet was garbled and the exact meaning must be confirmed in the datasheet. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf)
- DSP speed: Linko's 07x product page says trigonometric and square-root operations complete within 100 ns. Per-operation cycle counts were only found for the sibling LKS32MC08x: 32/16-bit divide in 10 cycles, 32-bit square root in 8 cycles, Q15 CORDIC sin/cos/arctan in 8 cycles. — [Linko LKS07 series page](https://www.lksmcu.com/index.php/LKS07Series/); [LKS32MC08x datasheet EN v1.89](https://www.lksmcu.com/static/upload/file/20230113/LKS32MC08x_with_built_in_6N_driver_Datasheet_EN_v1.89.pdf)
- Variants: LKS32MC070FL, 071DO, 074D, 074DO and 076F integrate a three-phase bootstrap gate driver that can drive six N-MOSFETs directly; a 3P3N-driver variant also exists. Peripheral counts differ between variants. — [LKS32MC07x with built-in 6N driver datasheet v1.28](https://www.lksmcu.com/static/upload/file/20230113/LKS32MC07x_with_built_in_6N_driver_Datasheet_v1.28.pdf); [LKS32MC07x with built-in 3P3N driver datasheet v1.12](https://www.lksmcu.com/static/upload/file/20230113/LKS32MC07x_with_built_in_3P3N_driver_Datasheet_v1.12.pdf)
- Document currency: Linko's download page lists user manual v1.69 dated 2026-07-11 and datasheet v1.40; neither could be opened in this session (v1.65 of the manual and a ©2023 datasheet were the versions indexed). — [Linko user-manual download page](https://www.lksmcu.com/index.php/UserManual/)
- **[teardown]** Dyson HD19 Supersonic Travel (teardown July 2026) uses LKS32MC072KBQ8 in QFN32 as master MCU, described as a 96 MHz Cortex-M0 with built-in motor DSP. — [ChargerLAB HD19 teardown](https://www.chargerlab.com/teardown-of-the-dyson-hd19-supersonic-travel-high-speed-hair-dryer/); [Chongdiantou HD19 teardown](https://www.chongdiantou.com/archives/1783912995960.html)
- **[forum]** Field experience on LKS32MC076: low speed-loop bandwidth is named as one cause of abnormal noise and over-current during heavy-load start, with dead-time adjustment suggested. Personal blog, not vendor guidance. — [CSDN, LKS32MC07 heavy-load issues](https://blog.csdn.net/qq_57471226/article/details/158964528)

### Inferences
- With ADC conversion at 3 Msps and DSP primitives near 100 ns, the 07x hardware is unlikely to be the limiting factor for a 1.83 kHz electrical frequency. At, for example, a 20 kHz carrier there are only about 11 PWM periods per electrical cycle (20,000 / 1,833), so the likely constraints are carrier ratio and observer/delay compensation in the vendor library rather than MCU throughput. (The carrier frequency is an assumed example and the arithmetic is ours; no Linko document on low-carrier-ratio behaviour was found.)
- The on-chip comparator → MCPWM FAIL route with a DAC reference gives a hardware over-current shutdown that does not depend on firmware; total latency = comparator delay (reported <30 ns or 200 ns setting) + digital filter (tclk × 16 × 0…15) + gate-driver/IPM turn-off delay. Only the first two terms are documented in what was retrieved.
- Dyson selecting the 07x family for a 2026 flagship travel dryer is independent evidence that the family is adequate for 110,000 rpm class products.

### Gaps
- MCPWM FAIL section of the user manual (chapter 14) was not readable: exact end-to-end trip latency, whether FAIL is latched or auto-recovering (cycle-by-cycle), and whether both BKIN inputs can be used simultaneously with comparators were not confirmed.
- Op-amp bandwidth and slew rate, comparator hysteresis options, DAC settling time, ADC trigger-to-sample timing and known errata/limits for the 07x were not found.
- No Linko-published hair-dryer solution on the 07x, and no Linko statement of observer type, maximum electrical frequency, start-up time or id = 0 versus flux-weakening options in the vendor FOC library, was found.
- Per-operation DSP cycle counts for the 07x itself were not found (08x figures only).

## 3. International state of the art: TI, ST, Infineon, Microchip, NXP, Renesas, Qorvo, Power Integrations, Navitas

### Takeaway
Among international vendors, only Infineon was found to publish a dedicated mains hair-dryer reference design (REF-HAIRDRYER-C101-6ED, iMOTION IMC101T, up to 100 krpm, dated 2020); TI, ST, Microchip, Qorvo, Power Integrations and Navitas address the application through general appliance inverters, name hair dryers as a target, or supply parts found in Dyson teardowns. The most relevant 2024–2026 advances are in the power stage and tooling (TI 650 V GaN IPM, Navitas GaNSense motor-drive ICs, PI BridgeSwitch-2, ST MCSDK 6.4 with HSO, TI C2000 with NPU), not in a published 100k+ rpm observer benchmark.

## 4. Built-in protection and diagnostics offered by these platforms

### Takeaway
What is documented in the retrieved material is the existence of hardware trip paths (comparator or pin into the PWM fail/brake input, cycle-by-cycle limiting, UVLO, over-temperature shutdown, fault outputs) and a standard firmware set (over-current, stall, over/under-voltage); quantitative trip latencies were found only for the Linko comparator setting, and no Class B (IEC 60730), phase-loss or parameter self-identification documentation was retrieved for any vendor.

### Cited Findings
- Linko LKS32MC07x: MCPWM FAIL can be triggered by an external pin (MCPWM_BKIN0/1) or by on-chip comparators, with a programmable digital filter (width = tclk × 16 × 0…15) and a comparator delay setting reported as <30 ns / 200 ns; outputs go to a preset safe level on FAIL. — [LKS32MC07x datasheet](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/lcsc/2404081728_LINKOSEMI-LKS32MC071C8T8_C7500649.pdf); [CSDN blog on LKS PWM configuration](https://blog.csdn.net/nullccc/article/details/149409725)
- Linko firmware-level protections listed for the LKS32MC07x mains vacuum solution: over-current, stall (堵转) and over/under-voltage. — [Linko LKS32MC07x mains vacuum solution](https://www.lksmcu.com/index.php/case_131/885.html)
- CMS CMS32M5710: enhanced PWM with a cycle-by-cycle current-limit brake-and-recover mechanism; dryer development board lists stall, over-current and over-voltage protection. — [sekorm application note](https://www.sekorm.com/news/40334306.html); [dianyuan forum post](https://www.dianyuan.com/bbs/2687252.html)

No integrated over-current trip was described. — [LCSC LKS1D5005D](https://item.szlcsc.com/21994647.html); [LCSC LKS1D5005DT](https://item.szlcsc.com/20520530.html)

### Inferences
- Every MCU found in a shipping 100k–110k rpm dryer is a Cortex-M0/M0+ or 8051-plus-hardware-engine device; no teardown found a Cortex-M4/M33, C2000 or dsPIC in a dryer. Compute class is therefore not what separates flagship from budget products; firmware, motor and power stage are.
- Dyson's HD19 uses the same MCU family and the same three-half-bridge-IPM topology as the team's design, on a universal 100–240 V input. The team's platform choice is therefore at parity with a 2026 flagship at the silicon level; any performance gap would have to come from firmware, sensing layout, motor or protection design.
- Teardowns identify parts only. They do not show which firmware runs (vendor library versus brand-owned code), so "Dyson uses Linko" does not establish that Dyson uses Linko's FOC library.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark comparing solutions (efficiency, speed ripple, acoustic noise, start-up time, BOM cost) was found; the dedicated query did not run before the search budget was exhausted.
- Laifen flagship models (Swift / Swift Special / newer), Dreame models other than the G10, and other brands (Panasonic, Shark, 飞科, 松下) were not captured.
- Shunt count (single, dual or triple) and current-sense amplifier arrangement in each torn-down board were not reported in the retrieved summaries.
- Part-number attributions for 小熊 A2, CONFU F9 and Midea come from roundup summaries whose originating page could not be pinned down exactly; verify against the original teardown photos.
- Teardown authors read markings from boards; none of the part identifications above was independently cross-checked in this session.
