## Key Question 1 — Which products are best-in-class as of late 2026, and what are their specifications?

### Takeaway
The flagship tier is still defined by Dyson (Supersonic Nural, Supersonic r, and the April-2026 Supersonic Travel) with Laifen (Swift 4), Dreame (Pocket family), Xiaomi/Mijia and Shark as the volume and value challengers; essentially every credible product sits at 105,000–115,000 rpm, and no mainstream brand has shipped a verified dryer above ~115,000 rpm. The headline motor speed has stopped being a differentiator: the differences are now weight (300–330 g travel class vs 400–700 g full size), sensing/heat intelligence, voltage universality and price (from under ¥300 in China to US$570).

### Inferences
- Auto-sensing wide voltage (no manual switch) is the SOTA handling method: Dyson and Dreame both specify a continuous 100–240 V range with no user action. No brushless product with a manual 120/240 V slide switch was found among first-tier brands.
- In a wide-voltage design the heater is the constraint, not the motor: Dyson rates the same unit at 1,000–1,220 W depending on input voltage, and Dreame caps at 1,300 W, versus 1,600–1,750 W for single-voltage full-size dryers. A wide-voltage product should expect to be specified as a power range, and its low-line (100–120 V) power is bounded by the 15 A / 12.5 A branch-circuit and plug-adapter ratings (Dyson's 1,300 W adapter advice is consistent with this).
- The target product (Φ27 mm, 110,000 rpm, 100–240 VAC) sits squarely on the Dyson HD19 specification; the competitive gap is therefore in weight (≤330 g), sensing, noise, attachments and brand, not in headline motor numbers.
- Single-voltage regional SKUs (Laifen Mini, base Dreame Pocket) are a known reviewer complaint for a product sold as a travel dryer; wide voltage is a genuine differentiator in the 300 g class.

## Key Question 3 — Teardown facts (motor topology, control ICs, power devices, capacitors, sensors, heater control)

### Takeaway
Two architectures coexist. Dyson's own full-size Supersonics (HD15, HD17) drive a single-phase V9 motor with a full bridge (two GaN half-bridge ICs, or an Infineon IPM) under a Microchip SAM C21 MCU; every Chinese-brand dryer torn down, and Dyson's own 2026 wide-voltage Supersonic Travel (HD19), uses a three-phase motor at roughly 310 VDC bus driven by three half-bridge IPMs or drivers plus discrete MOSFETs under a low-cost Cortex-M0 or 8051-class motor MCU. No teardown mentions a PFC stage; the bus is a rectifier plus 82–156 µF of 400 V electrolytic capacitance, and the heater is phase- or cycle-controlled by one or two triacs through optocouplers.

### Inferences
- Three half-bridge power devices imply a three-phase motor in HD19, whereas HD15's two GaN half-bridges and the 2018 unit's two IRSM808 half-bridge modules imply a single-phase full bridge. Dyson therefore appears to have switched its newest, wide-voltage, outsourced model to the same three-phase / three-IPM architecture the Chinese supply chain uses. If the ChargerLAB motor attribution is right, HD19 also does not use an in-house Dyson motor.
- For the team's product, the HD19 bill of materials is the single most relevant benchmark: LKS Cortex-M0 MCU, 3 × 500 V-class half-bridge IPM, ~156 µF / 400 V bus, 2 triacs + 2 optocouplers, 1 glass NTC, non-isolated buck auxiliary supply. The team's design is "SOTA-equivalent" on electronics if it matches this and adds wide-voltage firmware.
- Motor shaft power across teardowns is 60–105 W rated (Xiaomi 60 W, HD19 70 W, Laifen SE 105 W, Laifen Swift 4 160 W claimed output). With a few amps peak at 310 V, 5 A / 500 V-class devices (AP5N50BD, SPE05M50T, LKS1D50xx naming) are the norm.
- None of the retrieved teardown summaries mentions a PFC inductor or controller, and bus capacitance is small (82–156 µF), which is consistent with plain rectifier-capacitor front ends. In a wide-voltage unit this means bus voltage ranges from roughly 140 VDC to 340 VDC, so the motor winding and field-weakening margin must be designed for the low-line case; this is a design inference, not a teardown fact.
- Two triacs with "grouped" heater wire in HD19, versus one triac in single-voltage units, is consistent with reconfiguring or separately modulating heater sections for 100–120 V versus 220–240 V input; the teardown text retrieved does not state this explicitly.
- Integration trend: discrete MOSFET + driver (EG27710 + AP5N50BD) → three half-bridge modules (CM45SHMT, LAS1M0761, LKS1D5007DTH) → single three-phase power-stage IC (SPE05M50T-C, CS5755MT, CRMT5004E1). Space-constrained travel models use the more integrated options.

### Inferences

## Key Question 6 — Market data: share, volumes, price bands, ODM/OEM and component suppliers

### Takeaway
China's hair-dryer market has matured and is shrinking in value: AVC online data put first-half 2025 hair-dryer retail sales at ¥4.35 billion, down 10.5% year on year, with high-speed models above 70% of the category and products under ¥300 approaching 80% of volume. Laifen claims three straight years (2023–2025) as China's online unit leader, Dyson has been pushed into a small premium niche in China while a QYResearch listing still credits it with about 43% of the global high-speed segment by value, and the supply chain is now so commoditised that even Dyson's 2026 travel model is built by a Luxshare subsidiary around Chinese LKS/BPS silicon.

### Inferences
- Three price tiers exist in late 2026: under ¥300 (about US$40; roughly 80% of China volume), a ¥300–1,000 mid tier where Laifen and Dreame flagships sit (US$150–250 internationally), and a US$300–570 Dyson tier. A wide-voltage 300 g travel dryer is priced by Dyson at US$299.99 and by Dreame at US$149.99–229.99; that is the realistic price corridor for the team's product outside China.
- With the motor, MCU and IPM all available as catalogue parts from multiple Chinese vendors and Dyson itself using them, hardware parity is achievable by any competent team; durable differentiation has to come from acoustic quality, reliability evidence, sensing features and wide-voltage heater management.
- Dyson's ~43% global share in the QYResearch teaser is almost certainly a revenue share (its unit price is three to ten times competitors'), which is compatible with Laifen's claim of unit leadership; the two claims measure different things.
